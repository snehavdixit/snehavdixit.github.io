Teaching and Service : Sneha Vireshwar Dixit

Teaching and Service

I believe basic scientific and mathematical literacy is indispensable for a capable, critically-thinking society that makes evidence-based choices.

The 'phobia' of mathematics and quantitative subjects is not innate or natural, and can be circumvented with pedagogical choices and strong student support. Why is it common for students to proclaim they are 'not math people', or have 'math phobia', when an analogous 'English phobia', or 'history phobia' is laughable?

I want to work towards world where people are not blindly afraid of numbers, and science is not seen as a 'smart person subject', but as a 'logical and dedicated person subject'.

Graduate Teaching Assistant : 2023-2025

I worked as a Graduate Teaching Assistant for introductory physics courses (calculus and algebra based, both), leading recitation and help sessions. I focused heavily on building student confidence and circumventing 'phobias' of quantitative subjects (which can be common in biology and chemistry majors, as I came to find out). Another priority of mine was to foster scientific intuition and the skill of 'deriving' instead of 'plugging and chu gging'. My students rated my highly in my evaluations (however, it is arguable that those who were disgruntled simply did not bother voicing their disappointment).

CMS OpenData Toy Analysis Outreach in Bengaluru, India : Winter 2022

Following my experience as a USCMS PURSUE intern, I used my newly acquired Coffea skills to design a toy analysis for high school students in a manner similar to the Quarknet Project. I was particularly excited to use Python and the highly graphical Jupyter Notebook environment to introduce students to the basic workings of particle physics as opposed to a more intimidating command-line interface.

I created the curriculum and projects inspired by the USCMS PURSUE training toy analysis. Students from year 11 in Bengaluru, India succesfully plotted the Z boson resonance peak for the dimuon process using CMS OpenData.

Regrettably, the notebook is now out of date, given its many dependencies.

Washington College STEM Sisters : 2022-2023

During my time as a peer tutor at Washington College, I noticed that women are likelier to be less confident about their aptitute for quantitative subjects. The unspoken image of coding and mathematics as a 'masculine' subjects deters and intimidates many women from becoming tech-literate.

STEM Sisters at Washington College was created to provide women and gender minorities a judgement-free learning environment to gain proficiency in basic computer science skills. In a world that is increasingly online, it is important to understand the tools that we rely on so much. We cannot just leave it to the boys, as they say!

During its first year, STEM Sisters helped women on campus gain interest in quantitative subjects. We organized basic Python workshops, and help resume-building sessions. Even after my time at Washington College, STEM Sisters continued to be a successful student organization.

Public School Science Education (Bengaluru, India) : 2022

I volunteered for the non-profit Curiouscity Bengaluru, which is an organization dedicated to improving scientific literacy among school-aged children in India. I researched and designed several posters on various scientific topics for primary school children at public schools in Bengaluru, India. I also detailed cost-effective experiments to demonstrate basic scientific concepts that are easily and safely reproducible at home.

It was another full-circle moment - I myself grew interested in the natural sciences after participating in science outreach programs conducted by Curiouscity when I was in primary school.

Math and Physics Tutor : 2021-2023

I worked as a Math and Physics peer tutor at the Quantitative Skills Center at Washington College. During this period, I developed a Mathemtics 'bridging' course to help students with weak high-school math backgrounds prepare for college-level courses, especially intro-level physics and the introductory calculus sequence.
